# Investor and Business Overview

[Back to documentation index](./README.md)

## Executive summary

Cliqero is a catalogue-led commerce platform that can sell access to many kinds of externally fulfilled products and services without rebuilding its commerce architecture for each category.

The platform owns/curates the commercial catalogue. Customers discover listings, purchase them, and receive durable entitlement to access the configured destination. Eligible members can separately participate in referral distribution, but referral economics are a growth mechanism rather than the storefront's primary proposition.

## Customer proposition

The public business leads with what customers can discover and buy. Internal funding mechanics, ledgers, hierarchy, commission percentages, and treasury operations support commerce but are not the product's marketing identity.

This matters strategically: Cliqero should look like a credible commerce business, not a wallet product or a money/recruitment scheme.

## Catalogue model

Ordinary users do not create commercial inventory. Catalogue creation and publication are controlled by operators and permitted catalogue managers. This allows the platform to control quality, presentation, policy, and commercial inventory while still using a generic listing model across different product categories.

## Productless architecture

A listing can represent software, a download, a course, an offer, a service, an application, a repository, a private gateway, or another experience. Cliqero owns the commercial record, entitlement, and access authorization boundary; the destination owns specialized fulfillment.

The business can therefore expand inventory without introducing a new core product architecture for every category.

## Referral distribution

Referral promotion is an optional acquisition/distribution channel. Authenticated users can promote eligible listings through attributed links, and qualifying purchases may generate earnings according to policy.

Referral rewards come from real commerce, not registration, page views, clicks, or recruitment alone. The platform remains commercially coherent if referral levels are reduced or disabled.

## Revenue model

For new catalogue-owned commerce, platform allocation is the remainder after actual payable referral commissions. Missing-upline allocations remain with the platform. There is no ordinary-user seller-credit branch.

Future revenue extensions may include premium integrations, business API access, advanced catalogue/distribution tooling, managed services, or subscriptions, but these are not prerequisites for the core commerce flow.

## Buyer value

Buyers receive a consistent place to discover listings, durable purchase history, account-owned entitlement, and a stable Access experience even when fulfillment occurs elsewhere.

## Distribution value

Promoters receive a catalogue of eligible listings to recommend, attributed links, earnings tied to qualifying purchases, and withdrawal of available earnings according to policy. They do not need to host or fulfill the underlying product.

## Initial market and expansion

The initial market can begin in Nigeria while canonical USD accounting and provider-neutral architecture preserve broader expansion options. New product categories should normally require catalogue metadata and destination integrations, not new financial architecture.

## Defensibility

Potential defensibility grows from trusted catalogue inventory, buyer purchase history, reliable commerce, entitlement/access integrations, referral-network density, conversion/risk intelligence, APIs, and operational reliability.

## Core business test

> Can the platform add a useful new listing, sell it reliably, preserve the buyer's entitlement, hand off authorized access, and optionally distribute referral earnings without changing the core commerce architecture?

The intended answer is yes.
